Pragma's official plugin index now has two more coding agents: **Google Antigravity** (`agy`) and **Oh My Pi** (`omp`). Both shipped as 1.0.0 today and are listed on the [plugin gallery](/plugins) beside Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode, Pi, and the rest.

## Installing them

Open the [plugin gallery](/plugins), pick **Antigravity** or **Oh My Pi**, and click **Install**. Pragma opens, shows the exact install command, and runs it once you confirm. You need the agent itself installed and signed in first: `agy` for Antigravity, `omp` for Oh My Pi.

After that the agent appears in every agent picker, including new sessions, new worktrees, fanout attempts, and **Auto**.

## Google Antigravity

The Antigravity plugin installs a native `agy` plugin that reports through Antigravity's own hooks. What you get:

- **Live status.** The sidebar shows when `agy` is working, when it stops, and the latest thing it said, streamed from its transcript as each step finishes.
- **Questions and approvals.** When `agy` asks you something, or stops to ask permission for a command, the tab needs your attention and the question appears in Pragma, so you can find the agent that is waiting without opening every terminal.
- **Sub-agents.** A turn that hands work to sub-agents only counts as done when the last one finishes.
- **Models and permission modes.** The picker lists the models `agy models` reports, plus Antigravity's modes: its default, ask before every command, accept edits, plan, and skip permissions.
- **Usage limits.** Your Antigravity quota appears alongside your other accounts in Pragma.
- **Resume.** If Pragma's server restarts while `agy` is mid-conversation, the tab comes back in the same conversation.

## Oh My Pi

[Oh My Pi](https://omp.sh) is a fork of Pi with a much larger runtime: subagents, plan mode, approval modes, and structured `ask` forms. Its plugin is an `omp` extension built on the same foundation as Pragma's Pi integration, with its own handling for everything omp adds:

- **Live status** for each turn, with the assistant's reply streamed into the sidebar.
- **`ask` forms as Pragma questions.** When omp asks you to fill in a form, its fields show up as a question in Pragma.
- **Subagents.** A turn that spawns subagents stays busy until the last one settles.
- **Models** come straight from `omp models`.
- **Resume.** An interrupted session comes back with `omp --resume`. omp only writes a new session to disk after its first completed turn, so a session that never finished a turn has nothing to resume.

Pragma doesn't yet show usage limits for Oh My Pi, or ask you to approve its commands. omp stores its credentials in its own database, not a file Pragma reads, and by default it runs tools without asking, so it has no approval step for Pragma to hook into.

## Twelve agents, one sidebar

With these two, Pragma has official integrations for twelve coding agents. Each one is an ordinary npm package that installs through its own tool's plugin system, so if your agent isn't on the list, the [plugin docs](/docs/plugins/agents) show how to build one.
